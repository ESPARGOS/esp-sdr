PR #9 hardware review — 2026-10-08

Historical review: the implementation and expanded hardware results are
recorded in dc-retune-all.txt. Statements below describe the review stage.

The central claim is correct on all four tested chips. Repeating the IQ
calibration on a frequency change radiates a narrow tone near LO + 5 MHz.
Retaining the PHY's IQ coefficients while refreshing DC removes that tone
below the detection threshold of this bench. The evidence supports revising
and extending the contribution, rather than dismissing its underlying finding.

I recommend separating the emission fix from the temperature-tracking fix.
The former has a small implementation for C5, C6, S3 and C3. The latter needs
reliable invalidation and synchronization, not just a remembered temperature.

Retune emission measurements

HackRF settings: RX only, 8 MS/s, 7 MHz baseband filter, RF amplifier off,
LNA 16 dB, VGA 16 dB, centre 2354 MHz. Each run had a one-second idle lead-in
and tail, with 40 commands alternating FREQ 2350 / FREQ 2351. The first command
repeated the initial frequency; the remaining 39 actually changed frequency.
Other boards were not being retuned during the main comparisons.

Analysis used non-overlapping 2048-point Hann-window FFTs (0.256 ms per frame,
3.90625 kHz bins). A tone event required a peak within 100 kHz of the expected
LO + 5 MHz frequency to exceed the idle wide-search-band 99th-percentile peak
by 12 dB. USB callback timing is approximate, so the event search includes a
20 ms lead and 40 ms tail around each command. All 39 baseline events on every
board were detected, which also checks the timing and sensitivity of the test.

Chip  Baseline detections  DC-only detections  Baseline median peak  DC-only peak
C5    39/39                0/39                -42.8 dBFS            -70.7 dBFS
C6    39/39                0/39                -40.2 dBFS            -70.6 dBFS
S3    39/39                0/39                -23.8 dBFS            -70.5 dBFS
C3    39/39                0/39                -32.5 dBFS            -70.8 dBFS

These are FFT-bin levels at the HackRF, not transmitted RF power. The DC-only
column is the maximum noise bin in each event window, not a detected residual
tone. No ADC clipping occurred in these runs. The peaks alternate by 1 MHz
with the commanded LO, distinguishing them from unrelated ambient signals.
Measured frequencies include 2354.9766/2355.9766 MHz for C5 and
2354.9922/2355.9922 MHz for C6; crystal offsets explain the small deviation.

C5 used the exact PR patch. The other three used the equivalent DC-only
diagnostic mode. Median command times, baseline -> DC only, were:
  C5: 9.99 -> 7.91 ms; C6: 19.53 -> 18.00 ms;
  S3: 27.07 -> 23.99 ms; C3: 23.56 -> 20.23 ms.
These timings are for 2350/2351 MHz, not the PR author's 5180 MHz experiment.

The final minimal patch was subsequently built and flashed on all four chips.
Each passed another 19 actual frequency changes without a detected tone and
15 CRC-checked IQ10 captures across 2350, 2412 and 2437 MHz.

An additional C5 comparison at 5500/5501 MHz showed a weak baseline line and
noise-only PR captures. The original broad-band 12 dB detector was not sensitive
enough to classify that weak baseline line; the strong 13 cm comparisons above
are the primary evidence. An initial 2.4 GHz run with higher HackRF gain clipped
and was excluded. No claim of zero emission over the entire tuning range follows.

Loopback and normal startup

The pinned C5 phy_set_rx_gain_cal_iq routine itself calls phy_pbus_debugmode,
phy_set_lb_txiq, phy_set_loopback_gain and phy_start_tx_tone_step, then stops the
tone and restores its clocks/mode. ESP-SDR is invoking an existing PHY loopback
calibration, rather than simply forgetting to request loopback.

The stock tracker additionally surrounds calibration with phy_dis_hw_set_freq_new,
phy_force_txrx_off_new(1), phy_pbus_clear_reg and a saved/cleared bit 1 of
0x600a9c18. A diagnostic C5 mode used those guards and restored the state.
In a same-boot full / guarded / full sequence, maximum tone levels were
-52.11 / -51.86 / -51.68 dBFS. Every run still had detectable calibration tones.
Earlier runs had different calibration state and different absolute levels;
the same-boot comparison is the appropriate guard comparison.

Fresh stock startup was tested by erasing saved PHY calibration through the
SDK API, resetting, and listening while ESP-SDR's extra IQ step was disabled.
Three resets on every chip produced a repeatable narrowband startup signal:
  C5: about 2436.977 MHz, 0.336–0.351 s after reset starts;
  C6: about 2416.992 MHz, 0.393–0.394 s;
  S3: about 2416.965–2416.969 MHz, 0.200–0.201 s;
  C3: about 2416.969–2416.977 MHz, 0.180–0.181 s.
The peaks were 40–58 dB above the adjacent spectral floor. Initial listening
near 2442 MHz on C6/S3/C3 mostly captured ambient Wi-Fi, so it was not used as
proof. Listening near 5505 MHz did not reproduce the author's C5 startup
observation; this was not a scan of every 5 GHz reference calibration frequency.

Thus normal PHY calibration also leaks on this bench. The tested guards do
not cure it. This does not prove that every conceivable PA/loopback adjustment
is futile, but there is no evidence here for a missing single setup call that
would retain silent, high-quality per-frequency IQ calibration.

Receive quality

For C5 with the exact PR, five IQ10 captures at maximum reported manual gain
were taken at each of 2350, 2412, 2437, 4000, 4500, 4800, 5180, 5500, 5900 and
6000 MHz. CRCs passed and no clipping was observed. Example DC magnitudes were
16 counts at 2412 MHz, 38 at 4500 MHz and 46 at 5900 MHz. These measurements
support the need for fresh DC while retaining IQ; they do not establish usable
RF sensitivity or image rejection at all those frequencies.

C3 and S3 also produced valid DC-only captures at 2300, 2350, 2412, 2437 and
2484 MHz. Maximum-gain C6 DC was noisier and varied roughly 88–201 counts
between frequency medians. Full IQ recalibration also gave large offsets
(roughly 129–193 counts), so this is not a demonstrated DC-only regression.
It deserves separate investigation if maximum-gain C6 DC performance matters.

Image rejection was measured using an external HackRF tone at receiver LO
+ 5 MHz, at LO 2350 and 2370 MHz. TX amplifier was off. A first low-gain pass
was noise-limited and was superseded by a stronger pass (TX VGA 25 dB, waveform
amplitude 32/127), with ESP32 gain adjusted to avoid clipping. IQ10 capture
CRCs passed. The test compares startup IQ coefficients first, then full
per-frequency recalibration, on the same boot of each board.

Median measured image rejection, retained IQ / refreshed IQ:
Chip       2350 MHz          2370 MHz
C5         35.9 / 34.5 dB    28.4 / 26.8 dB
C6         39.9 / 37.1 dB    40.0 / 38.2 dB
S3         34.6 / 36.7 dB    31.9 / 31.7 dB
C3         29.4 / 26.4 dB    36.3 / 36.4 dB

Several image measurements approach the noise floor, received gain was not
identical in every comparison, and ambient interference caused outliers.
These results show no consistent large benefit from refreshing IQ in these
two 13 cm cases. Differences of a few dB should not be overinterpreted.
The author's particular 4/5 GHz image-rejection numbers were not independently
validated. No long-term thermal/image-rejection characterization was performed.

Temperature recovery and a better invalidation mechanism

The separate stale-DC problem is real. A C5 tracker calibration at its reference
frequencies overwrote the useful offsets for 4500 MHz. With recovery disabled,
captures showed DC magnitudes around 453–532 counts instead of tens of counts.
The PR's temperature marker restored good offsets when its stored value changed.

A controlled diagnostic test forced the vendor routine to execute by perturbing
its current-temperature input by 31 units. The routine performs actual DC/IQ
calibration and records its final measured temperature. That final, quantized
temperature sometimes equals the previous stamp despite fresh calibration.
Across 20 events with the PR-style temperature check, 10 had an unchanged stamp
and all 10 escaped invalidation; the largest resulting DC magnitude was 514.

A prototype instead increments a generation counter when the vendor's
phy_set_rx_gain_cal_dc runs outside ESP-SDR's measurement interval. It also uses
the SDK's PHY mutex around ESP-SDR recalibration, preventing the timer from
using measurement_mhz while that override is active. In 20 forced events it
missed none, including 14 with unchanged temperature stamps; post-recovery DC
ranged from 6 to 52 counts. Both strategies in this comparison used the same
locked diagnostic build, isolating the invalidation criterion.

This is a forced-calibration robustness test, not a measurement that 50% of
normal temperature-triggered runs fail. Natural tracking has a temperature
threshold, so its ordinary probability of producing an unchanged final stamp
was not established. The generation counter is nevertheless a more direct
record of the state change than a temperature proxy.

The generation-counter prototype is not a finished concurrency fix. The SDK
timer holds phy_get_lock() while tracking; production receive preparation,
capture and long-running streams need a deliberate policy for exclusion and
invalidation. Locking recalibration alone does not stop the tracker from
changing hardware during an unprotected capture. The PR also leaves the
measurement_mhz race unresolved. Its stale check is inside the rx_ready branch;
checking invalidation independently would make same-frequency FREQ/LPF handling
clearer, even though a subsequent capture can detect and repair the stale state.

Recommended change and validation

Accept the DC-only retune principle and cover the tested chips together:
  C5: retain the IQ-valid state, omit the two IQ calibration calls;
  C6: preserve flag 0x400 and omit rxiq_cal_init;
  C3/S3: clear only 0x200, retaining the 0x400 IQ-valid flag that makes
         set_rx_gain_param skip its transmitting IQ step.
DC remains measured at the requested LO; this does not restrict tuning or DC
calibration to Wi-Fi channels. If needed, explicit IQ recalibration could remain
an opt-in diagnostic action, with its actual transmission behavior documented.

The patch in artifacts/pr9-review/dc-only-four-targets.patch implements that
small four-chip change and updates the existing C5 test and documentation.
All four pinned-SDK builds succeeded. The base, exact PR, and minimal candidate
each passed all 50 host tests. The candidate also passed the
hardware checks described above. It deliberately does not include temperature
recovery; that is a separate known problem, not solved by this minimal patch.
Other supported families still need their own qualified adaptation/testing.
Neither this patch nor PR #9 makes startup/background PHY calibration silent.
